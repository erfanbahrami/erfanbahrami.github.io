Explanation-Triggered Response: XAI-Gated Autonomous Threat Response for IIoT Intrusion Detection

Shokooh Khandan, Erfan Bahrami, and Tooska Dargahi.
The 3nd International Workshop on Autonomous Cybersecurity (AutonomousCyber 2026), 2026
Abstract

Autonomous threat response in Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) environments requires systems that operate at machine speed while remaining trustworthy and proportionate in their actions. Existing intrusion detection systems either rely on human intervention for response or automate actions based solely on prediction confidence, without verifying the reliability of the underlying model reasoning. To bridge this gap, this paper proposes Explanation-Triggered Response (ETR), a framework that leverages SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) attribution distributions as a pre-action governance signal. ETR authorises autonomous responses only when classifier reasoning is stable and calibrates response severity according to the strength of the explanatory evidence. The framework comprises four stages: (1) an XGBoost classifier with TreeSHAP extraction, (2) a SHAP-based drift monitor, (3) a confidence gate, and (4) a bounded response actuator. It also produces a structured pre-execution audit record for each decision. We evaluate ETR on a publicly available IIoT intrusion detection dataset with 820,834 samples across 10 attack categories. ETR achieves 99.55\% accuracy and a macro F1 score of 0.9792, incurs no false escalations on clean data, and demonstrates severity-proportionate action selection. Under injected drift, the confidence gate improves response accuracy by 2–5 percentage points by filtering low-confidence predictions, without requiring activation of the SHAP drift gate.
